# rholo invariant inventory

The catalog of mathematical invariants the Rust rewrite must preserve. Each row links a law to its
source-of-truth location, its Lean formalization, and its Rust type realization; a human-facing
walkthrough of each law → the concrete Rust file/type/function that carries it (and the test that
gates it) is in [`../docs/src/contributor/laws-to-rust.md`](../docs/src/contributor/laws-to-rust.md).

**Scope note.** This long-form table ends at **Law 77**. The register
([`LAWS.md`](LAWS.md), emitted from `Rchain/Laws.lean` and refused stale by the gate) is the authority
for **Laws 1–91**: rows 78–91 — the developer allocation and its vesting, root publication, the
verified-answer join, the validator lifecycle, the trust model, the genesis anchor, the fringe state,
admission/concurrency (86), the decimal value algebra (87), block disposition (88), PoS liveness
enforcement (89), the published node hostlist (90), and the collection/censure charge magnitudes
(91) — are in the register and read in
[`../docs/src/formal/laws.md`](../docs/src/formal/laws.md).
Backfilling them here is open work; where this page and the register disagree, the register is right.

**Type system.** The port's own type discipline — the ρ-calculus embedded as the base sort of a
Calculus of Constructions, with no silent partiality — is specified in [`TYPE-SYSTEM.md`](TYPE-SYSTEM.md),
guided by `Rchain/Rho.lean` and `Rchain/Ty.lean`. It is **not** a new law and does not pre-empt Laws
the calculus rows (1–29) below.

**Concurrency model.** How Laws 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 19 combine to allow concurrent reduction — and
what must serialize, and why — is specified in
[`../docs/src/formal/concurrency.md`](../docs/src/formal/concurrency.md). Its soundness
theorems (diamond/linearization, disjoint-commute, sharded-scheduler, replay determinism) are the target
of `Rchain/Concurrent.lean`. **Effect-level correction:** the "disjoint-channel" reading of Law 9 is
*insufficient* — sound concurrent *effect* scheduling requires disjoint **continuation closures**, not
disjoint footprints; the channel-sharded scheduler is therefore unsound (see `Rchain/Effect.lean`, the
proved counterexample `effect_reorder_diverges`, and `docs/src/formal/scheduling.md` S.3/S.4).
Without further discipline the reducer's only sound parallelism is Level 1 (pure-resolution fork-join).
**Scheduler expansion (Laws 20–22)** lifts that ceiling: a per-channel claim queue that commits ops in
DFS path order (Law 20, "1 channel = 1 logical task"), the sequential-equivalent gate scheduler (Law 21),
and dispatch-time closure computability (Law 22) are the only sound concurrent effect schedulers — see
`Rchain/Scheduler.lean` and
[`../docs/src/formal/scheduling.md`](../docs/src/formal/scheduling.md).
The **on-chain extension (Laws 23–25)** makes the relaxed interleaving sound for the block path by
**validated speculation**: commits may reorder iff each validates DFS-order serializability (Law 24's
versioned write-record layer with prefix visibility), invalidated subtrees abort and re-run under the
gate, so the published log is the sequential fold's — see `Rchain/SchedulerOnchain.lean` and
[`../docs/src/formal/scheduling.md`](../docs/src/formal/scheduling.md).
The **cross-shard extension (Laws 26–29)** lifts that discipline from one shard to many: shard scope
is deterministic (Law 26, a typed `ShardId`), and a cross-shard transaction is a **two-phase commit** —
a Git-pull-request flow (propose → stage → review → merge, or close) whose coordinator commits on
every participant or aborts on every one (Law 27), with idempotent legs (Law 28) and a durable,
content-addressed decision record (Law 29) — see `Rchain/CrossShard.lean` and
[`../docs/src/formal/cross-shard-transactions.md`](../docs/src/formal/cross-shard-transactions.md).

**Status legend**

`spec/LAWS.md` and `spec/laws.tsv` are the *checked* rendering of the same rows (emitted from
`Rchain/Laws.lean` and refused stale by the gate); these cells are prose, and where the two disagree the
register is right.

- **proven** — proved in `spec/Rchain/`, over the model; the register distinguishes `proved-tied`
  (proved *and* tied to the node by a conformance corpus) from `proved-model`
- **owed** — the model exists and the proof does not (or, where a note says so, the statement is not yet
  the code's and a counterexample is proved instead)
- **vacuous** — proved, but the statement restates its own definition and cannot fail
- **stated** — theorem statement exists in Lean (Phase 0), proof is a later obligation
- **Phase N** — to be formalized and proven in phase N (1 = execution core, 2 = RSpace, 3 = Rosette,
  4 = Casper, 5 = crypto/storage)
- **axiom** — postulated (cryptographic primitive), not proven, by design
- **residual axiom** — a proof obligation currently declared `axiom` (not by design). The register's
  <!-- counts:axioms -->10 axioms<!-- counts:end --> are nine cryptographic primitives (Law 19, by
  design) and one residual: `hashHashes`, which Law 7 rests on. Law 1's comparator lawfulness is no
  longer residual — it is proved (the row records the four that became theorems on 2026-09-24)
- **open** — recorded question, not yet formalizable
